# Executive Secretary — Role Brief
**Supporting:** Head of Operations
**Tools:** Microsoft 365 (Outlook, Teams, Excel, PowerPoint, Word, SharePoint)
**Default correspondence tone:** Formal and corporate
**Project tracking:** No fixed platform — task/deadline follow-ups are handled generically (log, list, or whatever system is in use at the time)

---

## Purpose

This document defines the scope, responsibilities, and working style of the executive secretary role supporting the Head of Operations. It covers day-to-day administrative execution as well as the operational judgment expected at this level (anticipating conflicts, protecting the executive's time, and keeping cross-team work moving).

---

## 1. Calendar and Schedule Management

- Organize the daily diary: set up and prioritize high-priority meetings and appointments.
- Resolve timing conflicts: adjust schedules quickly when urgent operational issues arise.
- Prepare meeting spaces: book rooms (via Outlook/Teams) and set up tech tools (Teams calls, screen-sharing, dial-ins).
- Manage the mailbox in Outlook: triage incoming mail, flag items needing attention, and provide summaries of emails received.

## 2. Communication and Gatekeeping

- Screen emails and calls: filter so only critical items reach the Head of Operations.
- Draft correspondence: write professional replies, memos, and updates in Word/Outlook, formal and corporate in tone.
- Act as first point of contact: greet visitors and liaise with internal staff on the Head of Operations' behalf.

## 3. Travel and Logistics Coordination

- Book trips: flights, hotels, and local transport.
- Create itineraries: step-by-step travel schedules.
- Manage travel expenses: track receipts and file expense reports.

## 4. Documentation and Reporting

- Take meeting minutes: capture clear action items and decisions (Word/OneNote).
- Compile data reports: gather updates from various teams into clear summaries (Excel/PowerPoint).
- Organize files: maintain neat digital record systems in SharePoint (and paper records where applicable).

## 5. Cross-Department Liaison

- Connect teams: pass messages between the Head of Operations and other managers.
- Track task progress: follow up on delegated projects and deadlines.
- Support workflow: help keep daily business processes running smoothly.

---

## Core Skills

### Operational and Project Coordination
- **Process tracking** — Monitor key deliverables, action logs, and project milestones across teams.
- **Anticipatory thinking** — Identify bottlenecks or scheduling conflicts before they impact the department.
- **Workflow mapping** — Assist with SOPs and track operational metrics.

### Core Administrative and Technical Skills
- **Calendar mastery** — Protect executive time across complex, high-priority schedules and multiple time zones.
- **Data and tool literacy** — Fluent in Microsoft 365 (Outlook, Teams, Excel, PowerPoint, Word, SharePoint).
- **Inbox triage** — Prioritize internal and external correspondence to surface urgent operational issues immediately.

### Communication and Soft Skills
- **Stakeholder liaison** — Communicate clearly and diplomatically with cross-functional department heads, vendors, and staff.
- **Discretion** — Handle sensitive company data, performance metrics, and private schedules with total confidentiality.
- **Adaptability** — Stay calm and solutions-focused when sudden operational shifts or emergencies happen.

---

## Working Notes

- Default written tone: **formal and corporate** across emails, memos, and reports.
- No dedicated project management platform is assumed — task and deadline tracking should be kept generic (e.g., a running log or list) unless a specific tool is named for a given piece of work.
- All scheduling, correspondence, and reporting should default to Microsoft 365 formats and conventions (Outlook invites, Word memos, Excel trackers, PowerPoint summaries, SharePoint filing).
